# Establish the case you are answering

## When to read

Open this for a new case, ambiguous reason code, partial disputed amount or payment covering multiple orders. Skip it for an isolated wording edit with adequate context.

## How to do the work

Read the bank's actual allegation and attachments before drafting from the provider's normalized category. A reason code classifies a claim; it is not a finding. Record provider, merchant account, dispute ID, payment/capture ID, currency and amount, raw status, stage and actual due time. Keep purchaser, cardholder, recipient and account holder separate until evidence connects them.

Build a short transaction map: disputed payment → paid order/line items → fulfillment events → refund or concession. Use explicit identifiers where available. Equal amounts and nearby dates can suggest a join but do not prove it. Mark the missing join instead of laundering it into a confident narrative. If the notice covers part of an order, calculate the part and state its basis. Ask for the specific missing notice, purchase record or parcel relation that changes the answer; do not demand a whole customer archive.

Write the allegation in one neutral sentence, then the supported answer in another. Decide whether the available material supports contesting, conceding, or requesting more evidence. A merchant's belief that the claim is unfair is not a substitute for the missing fact.

## Name the process before choosing evidence

One unhappy buyer can produce several records, each with its own decision-maker, deadline and effect on money:

- **Inquiry**: the bank asks a question before a chargeback. Answer it; an unanswered or escalated inquiry can become a chargeback that needs a fresh response.
- **Chargeback**: the issuer has pulled the funds back, and fees may apply. A representment answers this.
- **Fraud warning or dispute alert** (network fraud reports, pre-dispute resolution services and similar): a signal or a pre-dispute refund opportunity. An alert refund gives up the principal to avoid a dispute; it is not a win.
- **Platform resolution or buyer-protection case** (marketplaces, wallets and some commerce platforms): the platform's own process between buyer and seller. Winning it does not bind the cardholder's bank, so a chargeback on the same payment can still be lost; a platform reimbursement can also arrive when the chargeback is lost.

Record which of these exist for the payment and keep their outcomes apart. For Whop, Shopify Payments, Stripe, PayPal or any other provider, read its current dispute documentation and the case page itself for which processes it runs, who decides each and how a refund interacts with an open case.

If the disputed payment belongs to a subscription or instalment plan, check whether further charges are scheduled. Charging again someone who is disputing an earlier payment invites the next dispute. Whether to pause, cancel or continue is the merchant's decision, but it has to be made, not inherited.

## Worked decisions

Illustrative: a 90-unit payment buys a 60-unit lamp and a 30-unit shade. A scan proves the shade arrived; the buyer disputes the lamp. A full-payment defense saying “order delivered” misstates scope. Link each parcel to its contents, preserve the partial delivery, and answer the lamp allegation. If the notice instead alleges two payments for one lamp, delivery evidence is secondary to transaction comparison.

## Deliverable and checks

Produce a case header, allegation, payment/order map and unresolved facts. Retain the original provider category/status. Use [packet composition](packet-composition-and-export.md) after selecting the relevant reason reference; use [deadlines](deadlines-and-submission-state.md) for live timing.
