# Decide whether and how to escalate

## When to read

Use for rejected evidence, pre-arbitration, an issuer's new explanation, or a decision to contest versus concede.

## How to do the work

Resolve three questions independently: is there an accessible route, is there a supported rebuttal, and is the incremental exposure acceptable? Identify network, reason, stage, account eligibility and the party controlling escalation. A provider may control or restrict access even when a merchant wants arbitration. Check current deadlines, fees, technical-violation exposure and filing semantics rather than using a historical fee estimate.

Read the actual issuer objection. Compare it with the original packet: was the allegation changed, a record missing, a join unclear, or a rule requirement unmet? Write a targeted answer with exact exhibits. If no rationale is available, say that; a loss does not prove the issuer ignored evidence. Do not invent a universal new-evidence requirement or promise that an unchanged resubmission will receive another review.

Estimate recoverable principal, incremental handling and filing cost, possible later fees and goods still exposed. Keep expected scenarios separate from observed results. Winning the substantive argument may still leave costs. Conceding a weak or uneconomic case can be sound; it is not permission to issue an extra refund while the dispute already moves money.

## Worked decisions

Illustrative: a quality claim loses despite a clear return offer. The merchant wants to escalate because keeping goods and money feels unfair. The better next step is to inspect the rejection and applicable return requirements. If the reviewer challenged the product description, more copies of the return label do not answer it. A purchase-time specification may help; if absent, retain the gap and compare accepting liability against unsupported escalation.

## Deliverable and checks

Produce an objection/evidence table, current route/access, cost scenarios and recommended next step, with an appeal draft only when there is something supported to say. A carrier claim or seller-protection request is a separate remedy, with its own conditions and later cash verification. [Provider contracts](provider-evidence-contracts.md) govern actual actions.
