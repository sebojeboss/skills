# Measure the additional value of recovery

## When to read

Recovered revenue claims, new discounts, longer sequences, timing tests or profitability questions. Don't require a causal experiment to edit one sentence.

## How to do the work

Decide what you're trying to learn and what you'll compare it with before choosing a report. A no-message holdout measures the recovery program; no-SMS measures the SMS addition; a reminder without the discount measures the incentive. These controls are not interchangeable. Some shoppers return on their own, so orders inside an attribution window are not all caused by the send.

For a timing comparison, assign eligible people when they abandon, before anyone has had time to buy, if the question is the whole timing policy. Keep their original assignment even if they buy before the delayed message and are suppressed. Comparing only the people still abandoned at a later send changes the denominator and makes timing effects misleading. Choose the assignment unit so one person's repeated carts can't cross arms, and note cross-device identity limits.

Use comparable observation windows and record other contact. Keep campaigns stable where possible, or define the test as the actual combined policy. Measure orders, realized revenue, refunds and variable costs per assigned eligible customer. Revenue already net of a discount must not have the discount subtracted again. Separate currencies or document the conversion basis. Missing cost is unknown, not zero.

## Worked decisions

**Why the fastest send wins the flow report.** Illustrative supplied facts, not observed results: 1,800 eligible shoppers are assigned at abandonment to three arms of 600 and followed for seven days.

| Arm | What happens | Buyers in 7 days | Per assigned shopper | What the flow report credits |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| No message | Nothing sent | 66 | 11.0% | — |
| 30-minute email | All 600 emailed | 63 | 10.5% | 63 of 600 recipients, about 10.5% "recovered" |
| 24-hour email | 45 bought before the send and were suppressed; 555 emailed | 78 | 13.0% | 33 of 555 recipients, about 6% |

The flow report says the 30-minute email recovers almost twice the rate of the 24-hour one. The assigned comparison says the opposite. The 30-minute arm did no better than sending nothing, because most of its credited buyers were coming back anyway. The 24-hour arm added about two points over no message, counting the 45 early buyers it never emailed. With 600 per arm, differences this size could still be chance; check uncertainty before acting, then compare contribution rather than orders. The lesson is how to read the comparison, not that 24 hours is right for your store.

**More orders, less contribution.** Illustrative arithmetic, not a measured result: 100 assigned people in each arm yield 12 orders with a reminder and 10 without. That is a two-percentage-point observed difference, not twelve incremental orders or proof of significance. If treatment contribution totals 240 currency units and control totals 250, contribution per assigned person differs by −0.10 despite more orders. Check which costs and refunds the totals include before interpreting them.

**The long tail.** A late attributed order doesn't prove the eighth message deserves to stay. Randomly suppress the extension for some eligible shoppers at that point and compare against stopping earlier, including other contact, unsubscribes and complaints.

## Deliverable and checks

State what you're estimating in plain words, then the assignment unit and time, eligibility, arms, window, order/refund join, contact contamination, cost bases and missing data. Show the numerator, the denominator and the arithmetic. Keep uncertainty and any design imbalance visible. Don't set a universal sample size, holdout share or statistical method without the relevant assumptions.

Use transparent local arithmetic or the user's existing analysis tools on permitted exports. Live assignment is a separate authorized action; no commercial improvement follows from a high attribution dashboard.
