# Use competitor history to choose the next check

## When history earns the extra work

Use Spyder when a shortlist decision depends on what changed: a returning product, a new bundle, a message reproduced across products, or whether an advertiser routinely prunes its tests. It requires the relevant entitlement, tracked Pages and actual history for those entities. Discovery access alone does not supply it. Check the account's plan entitlement rather than assuming a separate add-on purchase. Lens is for the user's own connected ad-account reporting, not this competitor history; neither Spyder nor Lens is required for core Discovery/MCP. Full recurring monitoring and funnel reconstruction belong to reverse-engineer-any-store; here history resolves a specific product question.

## Establish identity and coverage

Read existing tracked brands first. To add one through the authorized UI, open Spyder → plus → search brand → choose folder → Add Spyder Brand. If absent, Add Manually accepts a verified Facebook Ad Library URL. Confirm the intended Page and allow collection to populate. Do not claim a public read route adds a watchlist entry or schedule recurring monitoring merely because a product was shortlisted.

Record the Page, observation date and earliest returned history. Earlier data depends on prior capture; an advertised maximum history is not a promise for this brand. Check possible regional, creator or founder Pages when the business appears incomplete, but treat domain or partner associations as leads rather than proof of ownership. A Page is not an ad account, and a missing Page is not a business that stopped advertising.

## Read launches and survivors together

Open the brand's Creative Tests and inspect a launch-date cohort; use Timeline with a fixed date range and consistent platform, status, format and language scope. Compare new launches with surviving creative families, keeping individual ad IDs. Foreplay's **Winner Identified** badge means exactly one ad remains active in the launch-date group. It does not establish a controlled experiment, common audience, budget or profit.

An advertiser that repeatedly removes other tests while returning to the same product gives you a useful lead. It does not reveal why ads stopped: a promotion may have ended, inventory may be gone, or a new version may have replaced them. Inspect the changed argument, product and destination before treating ad-count movement as a product decision. Repeated media or crops do not constitute independent tests.

If the available interface exposes rankings, retain the ranking date, comparison window and category. “Breakout” and “evergreen” are Foreplay's ranking buckets, not merchant sales results. For REST, the [documented ranking overview](https://docs.foreplay.co/) uses `/api/spyder/brand/rankings/{page_id}/top-overview` with required `date` and optional `compareDaysAgo`; the related list route accepts `category`. These reads require a tracked Page. A 403 can therefore be an untracked-Page condition, not an authentication problem. Use actual MCP schemas rather than those route names as guessed tools.

## Follow the offer change

For a seasonal question, compare equivalent periods with inactive ads included and verify coverage for both. Separate a returning promotion from a continuously published offer and a new ad ID reusing old copy. Language, currency and regional URLs are clues to localization; they do not establish targeting country.

Open Landing Pages and inspect destination ad counts and previews, then open the actual destination. A commonly used URL deserves inspection; its share of captured ads is not traffic share, conversion rate or spend. Preserve query parameters that may select bundles or offers. A screenshot cannot establish cart defaults, checkout totals or every same-URL revision.

Return one useful contrast: which product, promise, bundle, subscription condition or destination changed, what the alternative explanation is, and what current store check would settle it. If the new offer only works with repeat purchases, an unverified subscription claim must not rescue otherwise weak first-order economics.

## Worked decision

Illustrative: a launch group ends with one active ad pointing to a two-item kit. The earlier ads point to a single item. The badge makes the kit worth inspecting, but the surviving ad could simply have replaced an expired promotion. Open the original ad-entry URL and compare its selected bundle, quantity, shipping threshold and final destination with direct store entry. If the kit is only economical at a supplier batch size the operator cannot fund, defer that configuration despite the surviving creative.

Keep a concise before/after record with entity, filters, dates, original links, creative families, destination and missing coverage. Retain analytical conclusions within the [API retention rules](foreplay-access.md). If the evidence requires more capture, hand off the specific question and limits rather than claiming a complete competitor history.
