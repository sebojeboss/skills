# Check exact SKU and quick supply feasibility

## When to read

The candidate is attractive but identity, variant, specifications, stock, MOQ or per-order fulfillment is unresolved. This is a screen and handoff, not negotiated supplier qualification.

## Find the item, and say how sure you are it's the same one

Search public supplier catalogs with the product's generic function words and a reverse-image search of the main product photo. Supplier listings often show "similar items", which are useful for variants and for a better-priced neighbor. Open matches and classify identity honestly: visual neighbor, seller-claimed equivalent, matching documented model/variant, or physically checked unit. Don't collapse those into "same product." Two listings with identical photos can have different ports, battery sizes, fabrics or dimensions. Record the details that affect the promised use: dimensions, material, connectors, power, components.

Matching dimensions establish dimensional agreement only, not full item identity, material, components or performance. A differing component in a supplier photo makes equivalence unresolved unless the difference is confirmed. Choose the most decision-changing next check without calling it the only missing fact or implying other properties were verified. A candidate can deserve first investigation on partial evidence.

Capture terms in context: supplier and listing, variant, quantity, currency, destination, listing date, stated stock, dispatch estimate and delivery estimate. MOQ is the minimum order quantity for this arrangement, and a custom package or logo can change it. A listing price at a large quantity is not the cost of one fulfilled order. Unknown terms stay unknown.

## When you can't find a supplier match

A failed search is a gap in your search, not evidence of scarcity. The photo match may be poor, the listing renamed, the item different, or the stock gone. What you do next depends on access:

- **You have a sourcing agent or supplier contact.** Send the product link and photos and ask for an exact match, a per-order price to the destination, dispatch time, and whether they can supply it without a batch order. If they confirm and others really struggle to find it, that may become an advantage, but only once it's confirmed and the terms are acceptable.
- **You don't.** Treat the item as unsourced. The trap is a test that works, followed by a supplier who can only fill orders through a batch purchase you can't fund, and you end up refunding the customers your ads found. Get an exact match and terms in writing before spending on traffic, or move on.

Either way, compare the operating model you need with the route on offer. For per-order fulfillment, a batch commitment can defeat the plan before any traffic test. "Someone could probably find one" is not a commitment.

## Samples, lots and revisions

A sample is evidence about that unit under the checks you actually performed. The next shipment, a repaired unit or a design revision can differ, and earlier good units don't vouch for a new order. Tie acceptance to use: what difference would make a delivered unit unacceptable for this promise? An approved reference sample (a **golden sample**) plus written tolerances makes that question answerable. A cosmetic fix can consume fit clearance; a working device can still fail in transit. Record the observed defect separately from the suspected cause, so that a corrected diagnosis doesn't erase a justified rejection.

## Worked decisions

Illustrative: two visually identical task lights list different charging ports and battery capacities. The demonstration shows cordless use, but only one listing specifies that function. Keep them as separate variants and defer the uncertain promise. Don't pick the cheapest photo match and carry over the other listing's reviews.

Illustrative: an appealing seasonal item is available only through a batch order whose arrival may miss the event. Defer that configuration even if comments show interest. Another supplier could reopen the decision, but delivering the first batch wouldn't establish that replenishment is financed either.

Illustrative supplied facts: a viral desk gadget has no match on the usual supplier sites. The user has no agent and $1,500. Keep it as a lead, ask one or two sourcing services for an exact match and a per-order quote, and set a deadline. If nobody matches it, drop it and keep the advertiser on the watchlist; don't launch hoping supply will appear.

## Deliverable and checks

Return an exact-item comparison plus a sourcing brief: required function, variant identity and its confidence level, dated stated terms, the acceptable commitment, unresolved packaging/route/stock, and the evidence needed next. Distinguish dispatch from delivery and listing stock from reserved supply. Supplier contact, quotes, ordering, sample and lot acceptance, and fulfillment belong to sourcing-and-fulfillment and their authorized scope. No certification, therapeutic capability or quality claim transfers from a similar product.
