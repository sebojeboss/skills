# Asset roles and shot planning

## When to read

Use before delegating an edit, filling asset gaps, or conditioning generation. A storyboard, a reference sheet and a model's starting image are different inputs.

## How to do the work

Inventory assets by job: product master and label art establish identity; style references guide appearance; a character reference guides continuity; a storyboard explains sequence to people; a conditioning frame supplies the image a particular model animates; motion instructions describe change; dialogue supplies speech; mattes isolate regions. Record which recipient consumes each. Do not upload the entire research folder to a converter that only needs one scene.

Map each script unit to the shot that makes it understandable. Include exact approved words, what must be visible, start/end state, source or missing asset, intended duration and editable overlays. Plan clean narration and reusable product/action footage before assuming every gap needs generation. Clip durations follow the selected tool's verified constraints and editorial needs, not a sacred shot count.

Review raw material before committing to a full edit: completeness, correct use, needed angles, lens marks, shake, noise, pronunciation and room for text. Noise may be repairable; a smudged lens or absent action may require capture. Keep transitions with reference timing when prose alone is ambiguous.

## Worked decisions

Illustrative: a shoe's three-view sheet helps align identity across a board. Feeding the whole grid into image-to-video may cause multiple panels or unwanted view changes. Select or prepare one clean starting frame for a single shot, unless the actual mode is documented to consume reference sheets differently.

A solo editor can hold a short assembly plan mentally. An external editor needs the missing decisions written down: selected copy, asset roles, intended ordering and what remains open to interpretation. More documentation buys less ambiguity, but a trusted collaborator may deserve freedom to improve execution.

Select and check storyboard frames before using them as production inputs; keep missing views and unsupported claims visible in the shot plan.
