# Provider selection and input contracts

## When to read

Use when a job genuinely needs new generated material or a model/mode changes. Existing adequate footage does not need replacement merely because generation is available.

## How to do the work

Define the required control before choosing a brand: exact product identity, one physical action, identity continuity, native speech, duration, aspect, local repair, or editable output. Compare only plausible modes on those requirements. Inspect current official input/output semantics, access, license, privacy and charging terms. A feature listed in docs may not be available to the connected account.

Before external inspection or submission, read [browser runtime and sessions](browser-runtime-and-sessions.md). Inspect the exact public mode/docs page and record the date; use generic queries and adapt product-specific inputs locally. Do not paste product context into vendor assistants.

Make a small mode sheet: required input roles, duration/raster/audio support, request/result identity, status/fetch path, charge basis, limits and uncertainty. A motion prompt can rely on a starting image for appearance; another mode may support ordered action timing. Do not force one universal prompt style across them. Verify what the actual tool consumes before submitting a board or reference grid.

## Worked decisions

Illustrative: a low-price model requires many retries for readable packaging, while a higher-price mode offers suitable reference controls. Compare total attributable work per accepted export, not just one call price. Without comparable attempt/repair evidence, keep the economic preference tentative. A model that makes an attractive scene may still be unsuitable for exact product operation.

## Deliverable and checks

Prepare the scene inputs and explicit missing capability even when a provider is unavailable. Use existing assets, a local renderer with verified resources, or refilm directions as useful alternatives. Before submitting, confirm the authorized inputs, recipient and budget. Retain request/job identity after uncertainty and reconcile before retry; do not convert a lost response into a duplicate paid job. Keep endpoint credentials and provider receipts out of public artifacts.
