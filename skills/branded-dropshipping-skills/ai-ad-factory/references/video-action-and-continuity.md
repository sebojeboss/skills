# Video action and continuity

## When to read

Use for physical product demonstrations, transformations, product handling or connected shots. Sampled frames cannot establish continuous mechanics.

## How to do the work

Describe the observable before state, action and after state. Identify the decisive interval: contact, engagement, deformation, flow stopping, release or persistence. Plan framing so that interval is visible rather than hidden by fingers, captions or a cut. Set acceptance against the action the ad claims, not simply whether the object remains recognizable.

Review continuity at several levels: object count and geometry; hand/tool contact; material response; movement direction; lighting and spatial arrangement; speech and scene state across cuts. Watch continuously when the host supports it, then use dense frames to locate a defect. Record text-only or sampled review honestly when continuous perception is unavailable.

Split difficult sequences into shots when discrete states and control matter. A continuous treatment may better preserve momentum or a fluid transition, but sacrifices local revision and can introduce uncontrolled events. A board is useful only if its implied state transitions are feasible. Preserve a clean ending when the action needs it; more detail does not compensate for a broken release.

## Worked decisions

Illustrative: a telescoping handle must extend, engage and remain extended after the hand releases it. A clip where a segment vanishes is not repaired by cutting straight to a beauty shot and calling it a demonstrated lock. That beauty shot may still be useful for a different purpose. Conversely, removing idle time before the extension can improve pacing without removing evidence.

A pouring shot that shows liquid moving can serve atmosphere. If the copy promises clean shutoff, it needs the stop and aftermath. These are different shot contracts, not different standards of aesthetic taste.

## Deliverable and checks

Return the selected interval, decisive timestamps, known continuity problems and intended use. Keep a valid illustration separate from a verified demonstration. Preserve the last accepted version when a higher-resolution challenger breaks the action. Escalate unresolved mechanics to refilming, different staging or a changed claim, not automatic sharpening.
