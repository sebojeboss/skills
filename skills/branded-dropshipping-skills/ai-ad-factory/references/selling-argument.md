# Build the selling argument

Read when writing or repairing an ad's body, including a static that needs more than a headline. For an approved argument, check that the execution still carries it; a crop or caption correction need not reopen it.

## Choose a reason to buy

Name a buying reason concrete enough to choose a shot. “Separate compartments” describes a feature; “reach the adapter without unpacking the pouch” describes a use. The angle is that buying reason, the format is its vehicle, and the execution is what the viewer sees. A product spin still needs an argument.

Build the body from the jobs the ad needs. Each line should explain a supported outcome, answer a relevant doubt or supply information needed for the next decision.

**Why it works, when explanation helps.** A mechanism is the plain reason the product works or differs. For buyers who have tried other fixes, a supported explanation can show why those failed. Otherwise, explain this product's operation. Split problem and solution only when both are supported and the distinction helps; the operation itself may be enough, and some ads need no mechanism explanation. Use language understandable on one hearing. A coined name supplies no evidence: do not invent a hidden cause, competitor defect or study.

**What the feature lets the buyer do.** Attach the feature to its supported use or outcome. “Removable divider” becomes “Lift out the divider to make a wider compartment” when the product record or a real test establishes that operation. If the ad promises to show it, include the removal and resulting space. Evidence need not appear entirely in the footage, but the depicted action must be accurate. Vivid after-states still need support. Usually the outcome deserves most of the copy; when fit or compatibility decides the purchase, the dimension or specification is the answer, so lead with it.

**Proof at the doubt it answers.** Place each proof beat where the viewer needs it. It can answer an objection or establish a belief needed before purchase: a clear assembly take can show that no tools are needed. Generic praise contributes little to either job. Use authentic customer accounts within their actual scope. A presenter who resembles the buyer cannot establish safety or performance, and study-like numbers need a clear measured outcome and population.

**A literal ask.** Say what to tap and what the viewer gets. A longer video can ask once when ready buyers have enough information, then again after proof for those who need more. The later ask can carry the actual offer and guarantee; include scarcity only when true. A short creator ad may need only a recognizable opening, the problem in the speaker's words, the product as the turn, a few supported uses and one ask. The speaker's experience must be real.

Write the promise, any needed explanation, proof and next action in plain sentences. Cut unsupported slots from the ad itself; a caveat in the working notes cannot repair an invented result. Build this common body before opening variants. An opening about retrieving an adapter can lead into a retrieval demo; an opening about carrying more equipment needs capacity evidence.

## Revise the decision, then the sentence

Review whether the copy gives a supported reason to act. Cleverness alone does not answer the buyer's question. Read aloud when available; otherwise identify the review as text-only. Fix stumbles, repeated promises, missing feature-to-outcome links and sections that restart an accepted argument. Stop when the next edit merely exchanges preferences.

Illustrative: “Testers felt more comfortable” leaves the outcome undefined. If a real study exists, state what it measured and in whom. Without that evidence, remove the claim and show a supported product use.

Illustrative: a genuine customer says a pouch kept their adapter separate on one train trip. Place the account beside the question about organization, preserving its scope. It supplies neither a long-term durability rating nor proof that every adapter fits.

Illustrative: a competitor ad explains a hidden cause before revealing its solution. Borrow that organization only if this product has its own supported cause and answer. If the honest story is simply how a divider comes out, let that operation carry the explanation.

Illustrative: a folding-stand ad has a long founder story and a brief clear folding take. If the buyer's question is whether it fits in a bag, prioritize the folding action and verified folded dimensions. Move the story after the ask or omit it; keep it earlier only if it answers a consequential question.

## Deliver the copy

Return the copy. For a review or production handoff, explain the doubt each proof beat answers and identify evidence gaps that changed the draft. When comparing variants, distinguish a new buying reason, a format change and a change of opening or speaker.
