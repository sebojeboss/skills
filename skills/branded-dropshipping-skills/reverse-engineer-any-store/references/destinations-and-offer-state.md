# Destinations and offer state

## When to read

Load when following an ad to its landing page, PDP, collection or advertorial, or when comparing price, bundle, subscription or checkout presentation.

## Walk the route the ad supplies

Click through from the ad itself, then compare a direct visit only if it answers a question; the two can show different prices, bundles or pages. Write each step as entry → pre-sell, collection or PDP → cart → visible checkout. Record the initial link, the observed final URL, locale, currency, selected variant and time. Don't invent intermediate redirects you didn't observe.

Routes differ for a reason. An advertorial or other editorial-looking pre-sell page warms cold traffic with an explanation before asking for money. A collection page lets a fashion or gift buyer browse. A plain product page with a good size chart and clear shipping is all some categories need. Operators disagree about which works best, and the same operator's answer can change from one period to the next, so record which route this competitor uses now rather than electing a universal funnel. Keep an advertorial's persuasive claims separate from evidence about the product.

## Read the offer state exactly

A PDP is the product detail page, not necessarily the final offer: quantity, variant and recurring choices change its terms. At each step record product identity, variant, units, displayed price and currency, shipping qualification, the default option and any subscription interval. Note what information is required before a price appears. A bundle may combine physically distinct items; keep the components and quantities. "Free" can describe the added item's selling price while shipping or return deductions still apply. A discount shown before you select a variant may not survive the selection.

Change one option at a time and take a fresh snapshot after each, so you can see exactly which amount or promise moved. Compare unit price and order total separately. Check the recurring terms against use: if a three-unit pack is a 90-day supply and the default renewal is every 30 days, the buyer accumulates stock. Report that mismatch and how visible cancellation is, without asserting churn.

Watch for careless-store tells, which explain weak results and show where a better store could win. Examples are a shipping fee that first appears at checkout, a throwaway store name, an unfinished policy page, or copy in the wrong language for the market.

Check message continuity. Does the ad promise an immediate result while the destination describes ongoing use? Does an advertorial present a speculative cause as fact? Report the seller's argument and the evidence shown without turning either into product efficacy. Remember that the buyer's alternatives include workarounds and doing nothing, not only similar-looking SKUs.

## Boundaries of the inspection

Routine reversible cart and checkout-entry inspection implied by the user's request proceeds without reconfirmation, even though it records analytics or session state. Stop before payment, account creation, sensitive identity submission, outbound messages or commitments the task didn't authorize. Don't enter a fabricated email or trigger abandoned-cart enrollment to reveal an upsell. If address-dependent totals are unavailable, keep that uncertainty rather than calling advertised shipping final, and never submit private customer data because a country selector asks.
