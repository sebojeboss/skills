# RFQ and quote comparison

## When to read

Prepare a supplier request; compare listing prices, returned quotations or route options; decide whether a quote can support a delivery/contribution assumption.

## How to do the work

Start the request with the exact requested variant and the real initial quantity, then destination and dispatch mode. Ask the supplier to return deviations explicitly. Request product cost, packing/handling, freight endpoint, service, packed weight, package dimensions, preparation time, quote validity and returns terms. Separate a one-unit trial from hypothetical volume scenarios. MOQ means minimum purchase quantity; it does not promise single-unit international dispatch.

Put every reply on the same unit/pack, material, size, destination and service basis before calculating. An omitted fee stays unknown. An included charge is recorded as zero only with its inclusion documented in the quote notes; do not double-count it. Compare cash commitments separately: a reservation deposit or annual commitment is not simply another unit fee. Ask which dimensions govern if dual units disagree. Do not silently fix the supplier's specification.

For repeated arithmetic, use the method in [order files and the existing stack](csv-and-existing-stack.md) with an available spreadsheet or calculator. Group specifications only after reviewing their actual material, dimensions and contents. Keep different service routes separate, then explain their speed/cost tradeoff. A cheaper express sample does not price ordinary customer delivery. Use current official records for applicable taxes or protection terms when needed.

## Worked decisions (illustrative)

A request says 20 inches/50 cm; the calculation reveals 50.8 cm and asks which dimension governs. Offer A costs $6 plus $5 freight and $1 packing; offer B costs $8 plus $3 freight with packing included. Known subtotals are $12 and $11, but B's different material or missing destination coverage blocks a comparable recommendation. Do not zero-fill unquoted charges.

## Deliverable and checks

A draft [supplier request](supplier-request.md), normalized quote table, known-cost calculation, non-equivalence flags and precise clarification questions. Sending the draft is separate from creating it.
