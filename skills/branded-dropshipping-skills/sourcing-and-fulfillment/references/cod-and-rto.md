# COD and RTO

## When to read

Cash on delivery, non-delivery reports, return-to-origin fees, or carrier-collected cash fails to reconcile.

## How to do the work

Separate the parcel and cash ledgers. For each AWB (carrier consignment reference), record order identity, expected collection, delivery/attempt status, buyer evidence, carrier collection, remittance allocation and bank receipt. An NDR is a non-delivery report, not proof that a driver visited. RTO is return to origin, not necessarily origin receipt.

Choose action from the physical facts: a genuinely available buyer may support a reattempt; a buyer already holding and having paid for the parcel needs a collection/status/remittance investigation. Preserve packaging/AWB evidence where it helps connect goods and cash. Check the carrier's actual action window and contract rather than adopting a tutorial's waiting period.

A confirmation process reduces unready deliveries only if it obtains real information. A surcharge payable at delivery is not money recovered on a refused parcel. Removing COD may sacrifice orders from buyers who distrust prepayment. Compare those choices using the merchant's actual delivery and contribution cohorts; do not call every unconfirmed order fraud or assume a successful prepaid route validates COD settlement.

## Worked decisions (illustrative)

Ordinary prepaid parcels worked after a carrier switch, but the COD parcel is delivered according to buyer evidence, NDR according to the platform, and unpaid according to the merchant. Reconcile the AWB, cash collection and remittance record separately. Preserve the possibility that removing COD reduces orders without inventing a measured effect.

## Deliverable and checks

COD reconciliation by parcel/AWB, contradictory-state packet, eligible action draft and missing remittance evidence. Supplier/carrier cash reconciliation belongs here; payment-network owns broader account settlement infrastructure. No unsupported accusation of theft.
