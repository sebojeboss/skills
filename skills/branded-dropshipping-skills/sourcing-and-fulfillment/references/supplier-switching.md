# Supplier switching

## When to read

Replace a supplier or carrier, add a backup, or decide repair versus exit after failures.

## How to do the work

Name the failure being replaced: exact-product fit, capacity priority, process capability, stock accuracy, dispatch, carrier lane or software mapping. Record the attempted correction and the evidence that it helped or failed. A faster reply may repair communication without repairing goods or cash. The diagnosis decides the replacement. A large supplier that keeps deprioritizing your small run needs a partner that values small runs. A capable supplier that lacks the specific process your product depends on needs one with that process, whatever its size.

Compare the replacement on the same variant, route, return handling and operating effort. A supplier that needs manual files may still be preferable, but include that labor and failure exposure. Stage the cutover: new unsubmitted orders, accepted orders, moving parcels, returns and open credits remain separate cohorts. Keep the old map/version so incoming tracking and claims still join to their original orders.

Define a discriminating trial when appropriate, but do not place it without authorization. It should exercise the actual variant and customer route, not only an express sample. A COD route needs its collection/remittance check. Use explicit stop/review conditions based on the failure mechanism rather than a universal failure count. If the user controls production tools or lots, read manufacturing-and-batch-transfer; a catalog reseller usually controls selection and order admission rather than factory release.

## Worked decisions (illustrative)

A carrier replacement removes repeated missed pickups but requires daily file uploads. Compare the saved delay with the new labor and mapping risk. A separate COD test remains unresolved even if prepaid delivery works; do not declare the whole replacement successful.

Explain the repair-or-switch choice, the new-order cutover and how open orders, returns and credits will remain owned. State what the trial must establish.
