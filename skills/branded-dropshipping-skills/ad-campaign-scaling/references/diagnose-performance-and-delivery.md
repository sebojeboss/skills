# Diagnose performance and delivery

## When to read

Load for a deterioration, stalled spend, surprising pacing, apparent funnel leak or contradictory diagnostic signal. Compare hypotheses against the particular evidence rather than enforce a generic audit.

## How to do the work

Begin with the symptom and its onset: less spend, worse acquisition cost, fewer recorded purchases, or lower contribution are different problems. Align the period with edit history, offer/price changes, stock availability and reporting lag. Build only the hypotheses that these observations make plausible. For each, name the evidence that would distinguish it from the nearest rival and the smallest useful intervention.

If spend is low, first ask whether the configured constraint was deliberately tightened. If it was, compare the foregone contribution of less volume with the risk of relaxing it. If spend is normal but sales appear late, inspect event/reporting definitions before changing creative. If cheap clicks accompany weak conversion, compare landing-page match, buyer qualification, load failures and offer economics; do not infer a single buyer motive from a ratio. A verified fault consuming money warrants immediate bounded action rather than a ritual waiting period.

**Zoom out before reacting to one day at small spend.** At tens to a few hundred dollars a day, a few purchases more or less can move ROAS from 3 to break-even, so one day says little on its own. Before calling a trend, read a longer window: several days, or as much spend as you can tolerate. Check what changed alongside the swing: an edit, a price or offer change, a stockout, a reporting or attribution setting, a checkout problem. Sometimes results settle with time; sometimes the ads need to be better. Waiting has a cost, so set the loss you'll accept while you watch. "It's still in learning" is a delivery status to check in the account, not a diagnosis.

**A ratio is a check-engine light: it says where to look, not what broke.** Expensive impressions that people click can produce the same CPC as cheap impressions nobody clicks. Cheap clicks with few carts can mean curiosity clicks or a page that doesn't deliver what the ad promised. A weak cart-to-purchase rate can be a failing payment processor, a shipping charge that appears only at checkout, or shoppers comparing options; split cart→checkout from checkout→purchase where the data allows. Buy from your own checkout, where authorized, before blaming the ad. A healthy overall conversion rate can make a low cart ratio acceptable. When cost per purchase already meets the target, stop optimizing the proxies.

Compare the plausible explanations, the evidence that separates them and the smallest useful action, including what would justify keeping the current setting. Pass a reproducible checkout fault to the store repair workflow.

## Worked decisions

Low spend and worse results have many rival causes, and several often arrive together: a ROAS goal someone tightened on purpose, a missing companion product pulling basket value down, too few conversions to read, marginal auctions that are simply unattractive, the wrong bid or optimization event, changed attribution or report timing, a promotion or price change, a real storefront fault, or a change in how the creative lands. Separate an account that can't spend from one spending at economics you won't accept; they need opposite responses. A cost target is not a guaranteed purchase-cost ceiling. A current platform incident is a hypothesis until separately verified.

Worked fictional Meta case: spend falls while a stockout removes an add-on and the merchant raises the ROAS floor. An “increase budget” response ignores two deliberate economic changes. Compare restoring availability, keeping the constraint and accepting lower volume, cautiously changing the control, or changing the offer. Later inventory recovery with no performance recovery weakens a stockout-only explanation; it does not prove an algorithm fault. Name the next discriminating observation and intervention sacrifice. Deliverable: concise hypothesis comparison with evidence for/against, unresolved confounders and a bounded next action. If buyer behavior is unobserved, do not invent their reasons for leaving.
