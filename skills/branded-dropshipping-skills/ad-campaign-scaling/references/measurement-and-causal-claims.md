# Measurement and causal claims

## When to read

Load when the user asks what caused a result, requests an incrementality test, compares conflicting measurement vendors, or needs to decide how strong a claim a budget decision supports. Ordinary operational decisions may proceed under explicitly labeled uncertainty without buying an experiment.

## How to do the work

Name the decision and exactly what you are estimating before picking a measurement method: keeping a channel, expanding it, selecting creative, or estimating an offer's effect are different questions. Identify the treatment, eligible unit, counterfactual, outcome, time horizon and minimum economically meaningful effect. Fix exclusions and stopping logic before results when designing a test.

For an experiment, specify assignment and analysis units; audit leakage, interference, concurrent promotions and changes in reporting. For a geographic design, inspect where exposure and purchases are assigned, cross-region customers, pre-period fit and unusual regional changes. Report uncertainty around the business threshold, not merely whether an effect is positive. If data cannot support a useful decision at tolerable cost, choose a bounded allocation with weaker claims rather than purchasing false precision.

For a before/after report, list the competing changes and label the result descriptive. For vendor comparisons, reconcile numerator, denominator, counterfactual and time horizon before comparing headline multiples. Keep platform-attributed return, observed spend-response differences and causal marginal returns distinct.

## Worked decisions

Platform attribution, before/after observation, randomized lift and modeled estimates answer different questions. A channel can be incremental yet have no room for more spend; a shutdown result says little about the next increment. Distinguish a test that discovers a promising direction from one that confirms it. Small advertisers can still choose decisions their evidence supports.

Worked fictional Meta example: an attributed 4 ROAS can justify keeping a promising campaign under a bounded operating policy, but cannot establish that removing it would lose four revenue units per spend unit. A proposed geographic test must account for cross-region exposure and purchase geography before treating regions as clean controls. An illustrative report claiming twice the spend, three times the same revenue and unchanged ROAS remains unreconciled if numerator/time/control definitions do not match. Offer a simpler reversible allocation decision when an adequately powered experiment is infeasible; do not manufacture a sample-size threshold from large-advertiser studies.

Deliverable: claim-strength note, experiment specification if useful, and the evidence that could change the decision. Do not declare a winner from a few CPA rows without the relevant comparison and uncertainty. Test setup/run permissions and actual experiment availability are separate from having read this reference.
