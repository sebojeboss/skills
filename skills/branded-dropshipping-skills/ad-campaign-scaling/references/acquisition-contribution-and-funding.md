# Acquisition contribution and funding

Read when profitability, target CPA/ROAS, delayed payback or available cash affects a Meta allocation. Answer two questions: does the next increment earn enough contribution, and can its obligations be paid when due?

## Calculate the acquisition allowance

Use a consistent revenue basis. Subtract product, shipping/fulfillment, payment and other variable acquisition-linked costs, including defensible refund/return estimates. Treat tax and shipping collections consistently in revenue and cost. Contribution before ads is the acquisition allowance before the merchant's required contribution; contribution after ads is the result. Neither is full accounting profit unless all relevant expenses are included.

A higher AOV raises allowable acquisition cost only through the extra contribution per acquired buyer and within the merchant's funded payback horizon. A store that needs first-order recovery cannot count a later renewal toward today's allowance.

## Compare the next allocation

Use a spreadsheet or calculator to compare matched-window contribution scenarios; retain the assumptions. Compare the average ratio with the change in contribution. Do not fit a response curve to two observations: mix, seasonality, attribution and refunds that have not matured can explain the difference. Attributed revenue growth does not establish incremental contribution.

Illustrative, observational arithmetic: spend of 1,000, revenue of 4,000 and variable costs of 1,600 leave 1,400 after ads. At spend of 1,500, revenue of 5,000 and costs of 2,000, contribution rises to 1,500 even as average ROAS falls from 4 to 3.33. If that second period instead brings 4,500 revenue and 1,800 costs, contribution falls to 1,200 despite a 3 ROAS. The observed contribution changes are +100 and −200; neither establishes causation without a credible counterfactual. Check comparable windows, cost mix and delayed refunds before acting.

## Fund obligations on their actual dates

Build a dated cash timeline from usable opening cash, payouts when available, and obligations when due. Include supplier and fulfillment payments, refund reserves, and any supplied debt repayments or owner withdrawals. Start from per-order fulfillment costs; include inventory and preorders only when applicable. Unqualified pending balances and long-run customer value cannot pay today's bill. Where same-day timing is uncertain, compare an outflow-first scenario.

Count proceeds when they become usable for the specific bill. Bank cash, card spending power and expected payouts belong on separate dated rows without double-counting. A funded Whop Card may cover an accepted Meta charge before bank receipt. Check billing currency, the next charge size, card limits and any pending-funds advance fee while keeping fulfillment and refunds funded. Earlier access narrows the funding gap; it does not improve CAC payback.

Feed verified variable payment savings into contribution per new buyer. Removing a monthly app bill improves monthly profit but does not automatically raise margin on the next order. Recalculate for the payment-method and country mix of the audience being scaled.

Illustrative: 250 available today cannot cover 400 due tomorrow because a 500 payout arrives the following day. Delay expansion, change a genuinely negotiable obligation or obtain an authorized funding decision; do not count the payout early.

Show the contribution comparison and dated cash constraint that determine the allocation. Maximum affordable spend is not necessarily optimal spend. Request existing cost and settlement inputs through the appropriate workflow, keeping internal economics private; do not request merchant credentials or public exports. Financing and fulfillment decisions remain with their respective workflows.
