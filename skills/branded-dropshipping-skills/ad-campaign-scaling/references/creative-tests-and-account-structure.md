# Creative tests and account structure

## When to read

Load when allocating a test budget, deciding whether a concept failed, organizing campaigns/ad sets, combining apparent winners, or comparing a page/offer/creative experiment. Connect discovery work to account allocation; creative production remains separate.

## How to do the work

Create a concept-to-ad map before judging the test: buying idea, format, execution variation, destination and offer. Separate large idea changes from cosmetic substitutions. Decide whether the job is to harvest current performance or learn which alternative works. Harvesting can follow delivery; learning may require explicitly comparable assignment and exposure.

Choose the economic unit before campaign structure: separate cases whose margins, conversion event, offer or fulfillment constraint require different controls. Do not split merely because every angle has a different name. Record what received enough meaningful exposure and what stayed unserved. Decide which unserved concept earns a bounded additional test based on strategic difference and cost of information, rather than declaring every low-spend ad a loser.

When considering another product retry, identify the change that could repair the failure and its expected information value versus testing a new candidate. A retry limit is an opportunity-cost policy, not a law of demand. Give established performers tolerance appropriate to their history without shielding a verified fault.

## Worked decisions

When the job is learning, adaptive delivery is the obstacle: the system gives most spend to its early favorite, so the others are under-tested rather than beaten. Launching several real executions of each concept reduces the chance that one weak execution hides a viable idea, but delivery will still concentrate. Neither one campaign per angle nor one consolidated structure is the default.

“Variable stacking” means combining previously promising components. A hook that attracts bargain seekers may interact badly with a premium-price landing page even when each looked good elsewhere. Treat the combination as a new hypothesis. A control is the reference configuration; a winner is a current operational selection, not proof of a permanent advantage. Horizontal scaling often means expanding through parallel structures, while vertical scaling usually means raising an existing allocation. State the exact change because parallel copies, new concepts and new markets are different interventions.

Worked fictional example: two landers in separate campaigns differ in authority cues and design, while delivery selects different people. Reallocating to the currently better result can be economically reasonable; claiming a causal design lift is not. A contrasting controlled test proposal would specify assignment and common eligibility. Another example leaves an unserved concept inconclusive rather than labeling it a failed product. For a causal comparison, read [measurement and causal claims](measurement-and-causal-claims.md).
