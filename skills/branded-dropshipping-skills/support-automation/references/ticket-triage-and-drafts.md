# Ticket triage and drafts

Use for individual replies, draft queues, macros and responder rules. A request category does not guarantee one safe answer for every ticket.

## Resolve the request before writing

Read the latest request and relevant preceding exchange. Identify each requested outcome and bind it to the intended store, order and item. Do not substitute the customer's last order for the order mentioned. If two orders remain plausible, ask once for the fact that distinguishes them; reuse evidence already supplied.

Address the request, give the decisive fact and available next step, then ask for necessary missing information. Promise a follow-up only when someone can meet it. Keep internal diagnosis and tool jargon out of the reply. Empathy should accompany action; repeated apologies and requests for information already in the records do not advance the case.

Illustrative: “The green item from my earlier order never arrived; cancel today's order.” Investigate the earlier shipment and check whether today's parcel can still be intercepted. One refund macro applied to the latest order would mishandle both requests.

## Inspect what a macro will do

A macro can change tags, close tickets or execute commerce actions. Inspect wording and actions separately before reuse. Make optional variables conditional and remove unresolved names or dates before sending. Keep separate variants for policies that differ instead of packing every exception into one reply.

For routing, specify trigger, store/channel scope, predicates, assignment and side effects. Make mixed AND/OR conditions explicit; inspect priority and competing bots. One substantive responder can coexist with an acknowledgment. Two responders making independent promises create conflicts. Route explicit human requests through the actual pickup workflow: a tag or view does not establish accepted ownership.

## Replies whose facts change the wording

These examples are illustrative. Use only facts established for the actual case.

**Label only.** A number exists; carrier collection is unconfirmed. Write: “A shipping label is available, but I can't yet confirm that the carrier has the parcel.” Add a claim that fulfillment is checking dispatch only after that handoff occurs. Otherwise keep the draft dependent on the handoff. A label supports neither “shipped” nor an arrival promise.

**The checkout promise was missed.** Supplied facts: the promised window has elapsed; the parcel left the supplier on 8 June, with its last scan at export on 11 June; a trace has been sent; an assigned person will check on Thursday; policy offers reshipment or refund if movement does not resume.

“Your parcel left our supplier on 8 June, and its last scan was at export on 11 June. We're past the delivery window shown at checkout, and I'm sorry. We've asked our shipping partner to trace it and will update you by Thursday. If it still hasn't moved, our delivery policy offers a reshipment or refund.”

Do not replace the checkout promise with a longer carrier norm. Remove the trace claim or Thursday commitment if either lacks its stated support.

**Refund pending.** Supplied facts: a £32 refund to the original card was submitted; the provider shows it as pending. Write: “I've submitted your £32 refund to the card you paid with. Our payment provider still shows it as pending; I can't confirm it has reached your bank.”

“Processed” would overstate this record. Bank posting time varies; do not promise arrival from this record. Add a follow-up date only from an owned check. Offer a trace reference only when that provider makes one available; pending acceptance does not establish its availability or bank receipt.

**Exception awaiting approval.** Supplied facts: a keep-item refund needs team-lead approval; the request has been handed to that team; no response date is confirmed. The standard route is a prepaid return label, with refund release at carrier acceptance.

“I've passed your request to keep the item and receive a refund to the team that approves exceptions. Approval and a response date aren't confirmed yet. Our standard route is a prepaid return label, with the refund issued once the carrier accepts the parcel.”

Keep the unconfirmed follow-up owned internally. If the handoff has not happened, remove the claim and prepare it. Use the actual store's release event; another policy may require warehouse inspection.

## Return the reply

Deliver review-ready copy with only necessary internal dependencies. For batches, separate accepted, edited and unusable drafts. Count review effort and metered integration work before claiming time savings. Inspect rejected drafts for missing context or wrong policy; do not send all customer history to another model as a blanket repair. Use [tracking](tracking-and-delivery-uncertainty.md) for shipment evidence and [pickup](notification-and-human-pickup.md) for transferred obligations.
