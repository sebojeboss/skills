# Offer economics

Read when pricing, quantities, gifts, shipping thresholds, returns or offer ranking could change the decision. Use actual paid amounts and contract costs; keep modeled scenarios separate from realized adjustments. Missing costs leave the comparison bounded or unranked.

## Calculate contribution per eligible entrant

Name the currency, observation horizon, original eligible entrants and orders. Keep exact intermediate amounts; round at the documented payment or accounting boundary. Missing cost is unknown, not zero.

Net collected per order includes retained shipping collected and excludes pass-through tax. Do not subtract discounts already netted from price. Deduct landed goods, pick/pack, outbound shipping including additional parcels, payment fees, variable service/support and applicable commissions or app charges once. Digital extras still have costs; subscription status alone supplies no renewal revenue. Separate contribution from net profit and cash exposure, and label fixed overhead excluded from the calculation.

Count charges as well as orders. A bundle paid in one charge spreads a fixed processing fee across more goods; a separately charged upsell can incur another fixed fee. Percentage fees still follow their contractual charged base. Include the cost of any additional parcel rather than treating consolidated payment as consolidated fulfillment.

Later net loss = refunded cash + added return/exchange/service expense − supported inventory recovery value or fee recoveries. Include reverse shipping and refund fees; avoid subtracting initial landed cost twice. A supported recovery credit can make this adjustment negative.

`cohort contribution = orders × (net collected per order − per-order variable costs) − later net loss − acquisition cost`

Divide by the original eligible entrants. Acquisition cost may be excluded for a specified question; state that exclusion rather than silently dropping it from a whole-route comparison. When repeat orders exceed entrants, use a matched horizon and explain the revenue/cost average; this is no longer first-order AOV. Count later renewals with their costs, not merely retained subscribers.

Use a spreadsheet or calculation tool to preserve the arithmetic. Show known subtotals, missing cost categories and the assumptions that could reverse the choice. Get fees, landed/parcel costs and return treatment from the applicable contracts.

## Find what could reverse the ranking

Illustrative: among 10,000 assigned eligible entrants per arm, 400 control orders at $20 contribution produce $0.80 per entrant. An offer yields 350 orders at $24, or $0.84 before later losses. Another $700 in treatment return expense reduces it to $0.77. Contribution per order misses the reversal.

Compare supported low/high parcel and return-cost scenarios, naming each assumption. Include parcel breakpoints. For a gift or shipping threshold, separate baskets below and already above it: an existing large basket can receive a gift or discount without any extra purchase. Higher basket value can therefore lose contribution.

For partial returns, check the actual refund promise before allocating paid value. Illustrative: list values $60/$40 and an $80 paid bundle give proportional allocations of $48/$32. Compare a $32 refund with an exchange that retains cash but adds shipping. Proportional allocation is an arithmetic option, not the merchant's policy or a legal entitlement. If supported bounds reverse the ranking, identify the deciding input.

## Carry the result into acquisition

Use contribution per entrant to judge the route. Added contribution per acquired buyer sets the additional room for acquisition cost only over the horizon the merchant's cash can carry. A store funding ads from its first order cannot count a later renewal toward today's allowance.
