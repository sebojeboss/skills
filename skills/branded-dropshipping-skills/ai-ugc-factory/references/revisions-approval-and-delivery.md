# Revisions, approval and delivery

## When to read

Use when feedback arrives, an editor receives source footage, a client asks for “one more revision,” or several versions and approvals need to stay distinct.

## How to do the work

Identify the exact artifact being reviewed. A note on a prior cut may already be addressed; an approval of vertical framing may not cover the square export. Bind the decision to a readable filename/version and, where useful, a checksum. A checksum identifies bytes, not whether they are good or whether a person approved them.

Separate four questions before recommending work:

1. What meaning, action, performance or presentation is missing?
2. Does existing material support an edit, or is new capture/generation necessary?
3. Was the missing requirement in the agreed brief, or did direction change?
4. Which files and use will the reviewer accept after the repair?

The first two are production judgments. The third concerns responsibility and agreed scope; do not decide legal entitlement from a fragment of a conversation. A technically small repair may still be new work. Conversely, the creator's own omission may require new capture even when the client calls it an edit.

Make feedback actionable: identify time range and current state, specify the desired state, preserve fixed elements and explain why the change matters. “More premium” is not an executable note. “At the adjustment close-up, remove the decorative text that hides the hinge; keep the voice and shot order” is. If the desired effect remains open, offer substantive alternatives with their sacrifices rather than generate arbitrary versions.

Check whether a small pickup can complete the story. It may need only a connecting sentence or a clear ending. Match context and recording conditions, and confirm how the new line joins. Do not promise a seamless pickup where the location, wardrobe or audio cannot match. A new location with identical wording is still new capture. A revised script approved before filming can still be disliked later; preserve that history without treating the old approval as permission to ignore new feedback.

Deliver files by role. Camera originals, selected ranges, clean assembly, finished export, stems and native project have different future uses. Include only the agreed bundle, with enough source mapping for later editing. State any missing fonts, linked media or project-format requirements. Download/access availability and retention periods matter: an accepted export can remain playable after the original project becomes impossible to revise. Never promise indefinite backups without an actual retention arrangement.

Record acceptance as a named reviewer's decision on a specific version and purpose, keeping residual issues when present. A client saying “thanks,” paying an invoice or returning for another job does not automatically establish approval of every asset. Publication, usage rights and commercial results remain separate. Carry already granted authorization forward; do not require a new ceremonial approval for every local reversible correction.

## Worked decisions

Illustrative dispute: a loose testimonial brief yields a story that stops before explaining the choice. Two new lines could complete it. The creator considers those lines outside the agreed work; the editor considers them the smallest repair. Preserve both truths. Prepare the exact lines and matching pickup requirements, explain the story gap, and identify the scope question for the parties. Do not claim the repaired edit worked until the actual footage and assembly exist.

Illustrative delivery: an editor asks for raw clips but receives an assembled version without captions. The export is technically sound and still the wrong artifact. Resolve the request as individual footage files with shot identifiers and usable ranges; do not re-encode the clean edit and label it raw footage. If the originals were not retained, say so and propose the actual available alternative.

## Deliverable and checks

Use [the revision note](../references/revision-note.md) for a substantial change; a small timestamped sentence can suffice. Return affected version, desired change, preserved choices, edit/refilm classification, available assets and unresolved responsibility. After performing authorized work, identify the actual new output and review coverage. Keep the previously accepted version until a replacement is accepted for the relevant use.
