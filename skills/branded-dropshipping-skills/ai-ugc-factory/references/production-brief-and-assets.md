# Production brief and assets

## When to read

Use when commissioning footage, planning a creator-style video, resolving missing inputs or interpreting a handoff request. Skip a full brief when one existing caption needs correction.

## How to do the work

Begin with the job the footage must do. “Show how the stand folds flat” creates different coverage needs from “introduce the stand while speaking.” Write the essential spoken meaning and visible action next to each other. Mark what is fixed and why: a verified dimension, a selected hook, a required destination, an actual product mechanism. Leave delivery and incidental phrasing open where exact words do not matter.

Translate each promise into an observable requirement. For folding, the viewer may need to see the unlocked state, the hand moving the hinge, and the final compact form. A beauty shot of the compact form supplies only the ending. If the script promises easy one-handed use, the demonstration must not secretly rely on a second hand outside the crop. If there is no real product evidence, revise the promise or request it; a generation cannot substantiate it.

Give the script a selling spine before you give it a personality. Most short creator ads run on a handful of sentences: an opening the viewer recognizes, the problem in the creator's own words, the product as the turn, two or three things it lets them do, and a plain ask. Category openers ("something I didn't expect to keep using," "stop buying this, get that instead") are common because they fit almost any product, which is exactly why the next line has to name a specific problem fast. Longer pieces add a demonstration and put proof where the doubts come up; [the selling argument](selling-argument.md) covers the body in depth. Write the spine first, then decide which lines must be said exactly and which the creator should say their own way.

Size the creator's latitude to trust and to truth. A creator you haven't worked with gets exact lines for anything that carries a claim, with the reason each line matters. A creator who knows the product, actually uses it and has delivered before can get the main message and room to say it their way; someone talking about a thing they really care about usually beats someone reading. Latitude still needs edges. Talking points with no required ending often produce a story that stops before explaining why they chose the product, and the two connecting lines needed to fix it turn into an argument about scope. Name the points that must land and how the piece ends, even when the wording is free. Freedom of wording never covers experience: a hired creator saying "I" about a result they haven't had is a fabricated testimonial, however natural it sounds.

Ask for the takes the edit will need. Two or three opening takes recorded against the same body let the editor build opening variants without a reshoot. A checkable shot list ("clasp opening, close; bag on shoulder, full length; contents tipped onto the bed") beats adjectives. For a transition or move that's hard to describe, link an example; "jump and the product appears" means something different to every creator who reads it.

Ask what the performer must do simultaneously. Count occupied hands, where the camera sits, what needs eye contact and whether the object can remain positioned during the read. Resolve impossible combinations before recording. Specify the essential coverage, not a decorative list of every imaginable angle. A solo creator can use short shot notes; an editor receiving somebody else's footage needs enough context to know which action and source range belongs where.

Define delivery by files, not adjectives:

| Role | What to request | What it does not automatically include |
|---|---|---|
| Camera originals/raw clips | Separate source recordings, including agreed usable takes and handles | An assembly, caption timing or project file |
| Selected clips | Named source ranges or exported selects, with source identity retained | Every rejected take |
| Clean assembled edit | Chosen sequence without specified overlays/music | Unedited camera footage |
| Finished export | Assembly with the agreed caption/audio/layout treatment | Publication or client approval |
| Editable project | Native timeline and required linked assets, fonts and dependencies | A portable project merely because a project filename exists |

“Raw edit” can mean an already assembled clean version. Resolve that ambiguity before filming or delivery. Specify whether voiceover and product sound must be separate stems, whether vertical/square variants are required, and what the next editor needs to change. Do not demand every possible version when one export serves the task.

Inventory actual product views, readable label art, relevant dimensions/mechanism facts, footage, approved script, selected speaker/reference images and music/font rights. For a real person's voice or likeness, keep a private permission locator and permitted uses/providers with the asset record. Having downloaded a reference does not establish those rights. For a fictional persona, record that status and avoid inventing its consumer history. Reference grids are planning material; the chosen generator may need an individual clean image instead.

## Worked decisions

Illustrative scenario: a brand requests “raw UGC” for an adjustable lamp. The creator expects to deliver a polished talking-head review without music; the editor expects separate clips of adjusting the arm. Both interpretations are plausible, but the files serve different jobs. Resolve this as a clean review export plus specifically named adjustment clips if both are needed. Do not silently double the job by demanding all rushes and a project after the price and scope were set.

Another illustrative brief asks for a spontaneous account of “how it fixed my headaches.” No supplied account or evidence supports that experience. Replace the request with an accurate demonstration of adjustable light direction, using verified product facts. This changes the unsupported claim, not the creator's identity or personality. If the actual objective requires a truthful customer story, the missing input is that person's authorized account.

## Deliverable and checks

Return a creator-readable brief with essential message/action, latitude, shot coverage, actual deliverable roles and review responsibility. Make missing product evidence or assets explicit. Use [the template](../references/creator-brief.md) as a starting point, deleting irrelevant fields. Confirm that every required action has a filmable setup and every “final” file has a defined role.
