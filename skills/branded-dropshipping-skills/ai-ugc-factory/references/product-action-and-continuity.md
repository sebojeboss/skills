# Product action and continuity

## When to read

Use for product handling, contact, label drift, material behavior, first/end-frame conditioning or a proposed edit that might hide a failed operation.

## How to do the work

Name the shot's job before reviewing it. A beauty insert can show shape or atmosphere; an operation demonstration must show enough of the mechanism to understand what happened. A plausible generated operation still does not substantiate actual product durability, efficacy, seal quality or a person's experience.

Inspect three separate things. **Identity** concerns the actual SKU: proportions, closure parts, label, quantity and relevant dimensions. **Action** concerns the ordered physical event. **Claim support** concerns whether real evidence establishes the stated benefit. A visually stable product can perform an impossible operation, and an apparently plausible operation can illustrate a claim without proving it.

Map the relevant states in source time: initial condition → approach/grasp → contact/transfer → release/stop → settled result. Not every shot needs all five, but a demonstration cannot omit the decisive state and still claim to show it. Note what is occluded. An object resting afterward supports its final position; it does not reveal every hidden contact on the way there.

Watch the whole interval, then inspect suspect boundaries. Check whether a label changes during rotation, a cap opens in the correct direction, a receiving surface actually catches material, fingers maintain a plausible grip, and the product settles after the hand leaves. Review joins for reset state: a capped bottle cannot become open between contiguous actions without an explained cut. Sample sheets can locate defects; they do not rule out brief failures between samples.

Choose a repair by what must remain observable:

| Defect | Useful response | What it sacrifices or cannot establish |
|---|---|---|
| Complete action with an overlong hold | Trim or retime surplus while retaining contact and release | Less real-time duration; unsuitable if timing itself is the claim |
| Missing or impossible contact | Refilm, replace the shot or generate a new supported action | New capture/cost; sharpening cannot repair causality |
| Label error on otherwise valid motion | Use verified art with reliable tracking/occlusion where feasible | Extra compositing; a sliding patch creates another defect |
| Wrong opening layout/count | Revisit initial frame/reference composition | A tighter reference may lose texture/context; motion controls may not fix frame-zero errors |
| Correct endpoint, invalid middle | Rework action or use real footage | Matching endpoint alone is not a successful demonstration |
| Decorative fragment still useful | Use it only for the narrower purpose it actually serves | It no longer proves the omitted operation |

Reference controls have costs. More appearance information may import unwanted layout; first-frame conditioning can preserve hands, framing or text that were never intended. Endpoint conditioning can encourage a final shape without a valid transfer process. Change the intervention that addresses the defect and preserve the comparison. A single attempt does not isolate cause, especially if seed, framing, resolution and prompt all change.

## Worked decisions

Illustrative scenario: a cable appears connected but never enters the socket. A fast cut might conceal the jump. If the shot only introduces the finished desk layout, the fragment may still be usable with accurate framing. If the message promises easy insertion, the same cut removes the evidence the viewer needs. Capture a real insertion or replace the generated action; do not score the hidden defect as repaired.

Illustrative comparison: a close-up of a fabric latch preserves texture but crops out the release. Widening the frame exposes the operation at the cost of surface detail. For an opening instruction, prioritize the mechanism; for an appearance insert, the close-up can be appropriate. The tradeoff follows the shot's purpose, not a general rule to always crop tighter or always show everything.

## Deliverable and checks

Record source/version, time range, actual identity/action issue, what the shot can still truthfully show, recommended repair and required follow-up inspection. Distinguish observed defects from hypotheses about their cause. Use [revision handling](revisions-approval-and-delivery.md) if a new shoot changes scope and [editing](editing-and-finishing.md) only where the existing action supports the repair.
