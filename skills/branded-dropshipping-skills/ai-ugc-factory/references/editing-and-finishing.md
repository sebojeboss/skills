# Editing and finishing

## When to read

Use to assemble supplied footage, repair pacing or captions, join audio, resize a cut or prepare exports. Preserve the selected message and performer unless their change is the actual request.

## How to do the work

Build a rough cut around complete thoughts. Remove the failed phrase after a restart while retaining the successful replacement. Check the join for meaning, mouth motion and room sound. “Dead space” is a judgment about an unnecessary pause in this sequence; it is not every moment without speech. Keep pauses that allow a reveal, a reaction or a difficult instruction to land.

Give each insert a job: reveal the control, show the operation, establish context, provide a reaction or support a specific factual statement. Match its useful moment to the spoken phrase. If the operation finishes after the line ends, decide whether to hold the completed state, extend the line's visual coverage or use a different shot. Blindly speeding every insert can remove the thing it was meant to explain.

For information on screens or labels, inspect at the actual output size. A crop can isolate the relevant control; a wider frame can preserve orientation. A keyframe moves between specified framing states, but it is useful only if the move helps understanding. Remove an animated zoom that causes a jarring jump or never settles long enough to read. A vignette can focus attention while also obscuring the very detail being shown. The useful decision may be to remove an effect.

Freeze an existing completed frame when the viewer needs to read it and incidental motion distracts. This works for a completed typed instruction; it does not fabricate the missing release of a latch. If the available footage never shows the required state clearly, capture a small insert or request one. Local editing is not always cheaper than obtaining the missing five seconds of adequate footage.

Caption the audible words, with explicit spelling corrections where appropriate. Preserve the source transcript separately. If speech is wrong, repair or replace the speech; do not silently paste the approved script over it. Group captions into readable thought units and measure them with the actual font at final size. Check punctuation, names, numbers, line breaks, edge margins and collisions with eyes, hands, product or other text. A separate opening heading may carry a different job from the ongoing subtitles. Use placement guidance for the actual destination rather than a universal coordinate preset.

Join sound as carefully as picture. Compare adjacent voiceover and talking-head regions for level, room character and noise. A visually smooth cut can sound like a different recording session. Keep original speech before cleanup and listen to the processed result for damaged syllables. Add music only when wanted, licensed and compatible with intelligibility. Do not choose a music bed as a substitute for repairing a large level jump.

Before sending a cut, watch it once at phone size with the sound off. You'll catch the misspelled caption your ears were correcting for, and see whether the ad still makes sense to people who never unmute. Then listen once on headphones for level jumps between the talking head and the voiceover, and for lips drifting off the words. If a tightened cut no longer carries the main message, it's too short, whatever its runtime.

Finishing starts from the native selected picture. Upscale, interpolation and synthetic degradation are optional responses to a particular need, not routine signs of professional work. A sharper face may become less natural; interpolation can smear hands across contact or manufacture transitions across cuts. Compare against the retained control under the intended viewing conditions. A desktop preference is not evidence of better ad performance.

## Worked decisions

Illustrative sequence: a quick settings tutorial speeds through selecting a toggle and immediately cuts away. Slowing the entire recording preserves clarity but adds unnecessary waiting. Instead, retain enough motion to locate the control and hold the actual selected state. If a zoom makes the interface unrecognizable, return to the wider view; if the wide view makes the control illegible, capture a clearer insert. Each choice trades runtime, context and legibility.

Illustrative caption repair: a subtitle covers the hand just as it releases a clasp. Move or reflow those events rather than shrinking every caption or redesigning the whole video. Recheck the new placement in neighboring shots; solving one collision can create another. This is a layout repair, not a reason to rewrite the opening or regenerate the performer.

## Deliverable and checks

Preserve source ranges, selected audio, subtitle file and edit plan with the requested export. Use [media editing and export](media-primitives-contract.md) with the user's actual editor or media tools. Check supported operations before acting; do not invent effect flags. Inspect final words, joins, caption events, aspect/rotation and the actual output—not just the timeline preview. State whether playback/listening was complete or sampled. [Delivery](revisions-approval-and-delivery.md) governs which version is accepted.
