# Rollout and resolution review

## When to read

Use when enabling or expanding automation, comparing support tools, measuring impact or pausing failures. Skip deployment work for a one-off draft.

## How to do the work

Select initial intents from actual recent tickets and available operations, not generic “automate everything” categories.

Switch on self-service only for actions the operation can actually perform. A cancel-order button promises that the warehouse or supplier can still stop the parcel. If the supplier pushes orders out within hours, or the 3PL cannot reliably intercept, leave cancellation as a request a person reviews, and say so in the flow. You give up some apparent automation coverage; in return you avoid telling a customer "cancelled" while the parcel ships anyway, which invites a dispute. Apply the same test to address changes, exchanges and refund-on-request: check the downstream system that has to honor the action before exposing the button.

Separate avoidable transactional contacts from educational or complex conversations the merchant wants. The goal may be fewer needless inquiries and better human investigation, not the lowest total contact rate.

Choose an owner with time to maintain policy, drafts, exceptions and integrations. Begin with internal drafts or a small supported intent where errors are observable. Review output against the existing process: factual correctness, editing burden, permitted action, customer effort, completed remedy and follow-up. Generated draft count is not time saved; add review work and orchestration charges to the comparison.

Examine failures as connected sequences. Was the source missing, wrong policy selected, wrong order joined, action unavailable, action partially executed, reply undelivered or human queue unstaffed? Repair that mechanism rather than merely switching model or adding more context. Preserve useful successes beside complaints; selected testimonials do not establish typical results.

Define every metric’s denominator and window. Deflection may mean no human reply; closed tickets may include silence. CSAT measures respondents, so changing response rates or case mix can invalidate a simple before/after comparison. Include recontacts/reopens, unresolved age, delivery failures and remedy outcomes. A fast first-response SLA can coexist with a long unresolved escalation; measure time to resolution separately. Do not impose a universal automation target or compare simple bot work to specialists’ difficult cases as if they were equivalent.

## Worked decisions

Illustrative: the bot closes more tickets while buyers reopen them after a refund promise. Inspect the refund and delivery chain before celebrating closure. Narrow the failing intent, retain working status answers and assign the unresolved cases to staffed pickup. This sacrifices apparent coverage while repairing actual service.

To pause, stop new admissions and identify in-flight work independently. Keep already-promised follow-ups and pending remedies owned. Reverting a rule does not undo money movements or restore lost messages.

## Deliverable and checks

Deliver a scoped enabled-intent list, owner, review findings and pause/recovery plan. Review representative cases using the existing helpdesk preview/testing surface or a small supplied-facts table. State the policy version, expected reply/action, actual result and remaining uncertainty. Keep language quality, policy applicability, remedy execution, message delivery and customer resolution separate.

Include a boundary case on each side of a time or value threshold, missing delivery evidence, a currency mismatch, an eligible request beyond the operator’s authority and an already-completed remedy. Preserve expected decisions before running the comparison. Do not change expectations merely to make a failing result appear correct; investigate the rule and facts.

Provider/channel tests need authorized resources and actual receipts. A correct draft or case table does not prove improved CSAT, notification delivery or a completed refund. Check the connected sequence before expanding the enabled scope.
