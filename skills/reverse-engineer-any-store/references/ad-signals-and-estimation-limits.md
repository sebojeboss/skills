# Read ad signals without inventing performance

## When to read

Interpret ad age, duplicates, disappearing ads, traffic or spend estimates before choosing which competitor to inspect.

## Choose the signal for the decision

- **Early entry or persistence?** If comparable captures show an advertiser pruning short-lived ads, a days-old ad that survives deserves inspection. Without that history, do not project your own kill rule onto it. A months-old ad supplies publication history but may be forgotten or lightly funded; check for copies rather than assuming them. Freshness sacrifices persistence evidence for entry timing. Neither reveals spend.
- **Repeated creative or a new argument?** New ad IDs using the same creative, new footage for one product, and different bodies under one opening show where the advertiser keeps working. They can reflect expansion, relaunches, audience or placement tests, or housekeeping. Keep every ID inside its creative family and inspect the differences. Public listings cannot distinguish budget increases from added campaigns.
- **Active or disappeared?** Active-only captures omit paused ads; caches can retain them. A business may use several Pages. Compare status, time and scope before interpreting a change. Disappearance can follow failure, stock exhaustion or stock moving to another channel. Missing data is not zero.

Keep the unit beside the signal: creative, ad ID, Page, account, domain, country and period. Then identify the missing link: publication to spend, traffic to conversion, or orders to refunds, costs and delivery.

## Inspect the estimate's construction

A spend estimator may combine public reach with an assumed country cost per thousand. Read its documented formula before using it: reach counts people, while CPM prices impressions. Frequency, period and country allocation must connect those units. Do not claim a tool omits those inputs unless its implementation establishes that.

CPM varies with objective and season. Active-only collection can miss recently paused spending, and one Page can omit other senders. Those omissions do not make the estimate a lower bound: an inflated CPM can push the result upward. Keep the provider, formula, assumptions and coverage attached. Compare estimates only as tentative prioritization signals where their scope and method are comparable; never report them as budgets.

A “best performing” label may mean highest reach, longest runtime or estimated spend. Read the ranking field. A reach campaign can meet its objective without selling anything.

## Use traffic estimates comparatively, with limits

Historical comparisons found useful ranking agreement alongside large individual-site errors in both directions. That supports relative hypotheses, not a guarantee that any pair is ranked correctly. Match metric, period and coverage before comparing sites; do not multiply estimated visits by an assumed conversion rate and order value to report revenue.

Small-site coverage can be weak. A blank may reflect insufficient data or a coverage restriction; inspect the provider's definition rather than diagnosing the cause or treating it as zero. No fixed correction factor resolves individual error. With authorized first-party figures, compare the same entity, period and unit, retaining mismatches.

## Worked decisions

Illustrative supplied facts: a tool reports lower spend after an ad disappears, while a cached capture still includes it. Resolve status, period and capture scope before describing the change. Neither capture establishes a budget reduction.

Illustrative supplied facts: an estimator covers one store Page, while three other Pages also send ads to the store. Report incomplete sender coverage and inspect those Pages. The estimate is neither the business's budget nor a proven minimum; the covered Page's modeled cost can itself be wrong.

Return the observation, its interpretation and the next check: resolve sender coverage, open the destination, inspect renewal terms, or compare authorized matching cost records. Use “worth inspecting because…” when public evidence is all you have. No paid tool is required.
