# Shipping, returns and followthrough

## When to read

Load for delivery promises, guarantees, bundle returns or public post-purchase reports from a competitor's buyers.

## Read the promise as written

Read the shipping and return policy in the same locale as the offer. Separate dispatch time from transit time, business days from calendar days, preorder dates from in-stock estimates, and free-shipping thresholds from destination exclusions. Record the policy date and region, exclusions, cash refund versus store credit, return fees and any partial-return formula. Read the footnotes and body text when the banner makes a simpler promise: "hassle-free returns" and "free returns" are different promises, and a homepage can say one while the policy says the other. Keep the conflicting wording; don't silently choose the favorable reading. Text extracted from a page is not a tested rendered checkout.

## Work the remedy on a concrete order

Bundle and gift rules only make sense in numbers. Record the paid subtotal, item values, gift treatment, retained items, fees and refund basis, and use exact arithmetic when it matters.

Illustrative: a bundle sells for 100 and contains items with list values of 80 and 40. Return the second item under proportional allocation and the refund is 100 × 40 ÷ 120 = 33.33. Under a rule that reprices the retained item at its full value of 80, the refund is 100 − 80 = 20, before any return fee. Show the assumptions; this is the advertised rule, not a legal entitlement. Note whether the policy leaves the fee or the repricing date unknown.

## Reconstruct buyer episodes to the end

For a public buyer episode, build a dated sequence: the occasion they needed it for, order, label created, carrier movement, receipt, complaint, remedy approved, goods returned, cash received, and later use. Keep author and reply relationships intact, and read later updates before classifying the outcome. A refund approval isn't money received. A parcel that arrives after the concert may not restore what the buyer paid for. A cancellation with a promised refund can still destroy the original offer: the limited edition is gone, the discount can't be reused, and reordering the rest adds shipping.

Look for completed recoveries as well as complaints. A stalled order can end with a refund and both the original and the replacement delivered. That changes the episode, but without a denominator it says nothing about typical reliability. A delivered product can still lack replacement accessories months later. The competitor's promise includes continuing ownership support, not only the first parcel.

Historical episodes need the policy in force at order time; the current policy can't judge them.

## Deliverable and checks

Produce a dated promise and conditions table, plus only the event timeline the decision needs, with unknown states shown. User reports remain attributed reports without authenticated receipts. Fulfillment design, remedies, dispute defense and legal interpretation belong to their own workflows, and the investigation doesn't authorize contacting buyers or staff.
