# Adapt a reference

## When to read

Use this for “make something like this,” format study or a source-based writing handoff. Study the actual example, not merely its first-line transcription or a label assigned by someone else.

## How to do the work

Open the reference through its first fulfillment and relevant complete body/payoff. Record what the intended viewer is offered, what the opening makes understandable and how the continuation delivers it. If a visual or performance carries the meaning, inspect that modality or mark it unresolved. See [source inspection](inspect-source-media.md).

Separate three kinds of reusable material:

- **Relationship:** a difficult task becomes approachable through visible small actions; a familiar assumption is contradicted by a relevant demonstration; a choice is reinterpreted at the ending.
- **Delivery structure:** count and duration promise, immediate item one, alternation between attempt and response, reveal followed by explanation, or progression toward a real outcome.
- **Surface choices:** setting, particular prop, slang, styling and incidental gestures. These may matter, but their presence does not establish necessity.

Map the relationship to the new viewer's actual wanted experience. A technically parallel noun may miss it: a beginner may want to finish one useful task, not learn the professional name for the whole discipline. Keep the useful promise and replace incompatible relationships. If an analogy depends on prices rising and falling, copying that up/down wording into a task with no corresponding states produces a fluent but empty comparison.

Carry the adaptation far enough to test its promise. A numbered list in a stated time needs actual useful items, a delivery rhythm that can fit them, and a prompt start. An impressive headline followed by setup about the filming location changes that experience. A source whose pleasure comes from a lively interaction cannot be preserved solely by turning its first words into an educational statement.

Examine the sacrifice. A very simple explanation can give a beginner a concrete first success while omitting a helpful exception. That may be appropriate for a limited promise. If the omitted condition determines whether the action works, change the promise or include the condition; do not simplify into false instruction. “Easy to understand” and “enough to apply” are different achievements.

Format study is practice, not a requirement to impersonate another creator. Preserve humor, fascination, emotional meaning and ordinary speech. Once the draft communicates clearly, check whether more mechanical edits would make it feel forced. Reading aloud can help; there is no mandatory number of passes or keyword swaps. Natural presence can coexist with preparation, and it is not established by removing all structure.

## Worked decisions

Illustrative adaptation: a repair video promises three useful actions and immediately demonstrates the first. The new task is a short packing lesson using supplied footage.

A weak adaptation copies the count but begins: “I love traveling, and after thinking about this for a long time, I wanted to share my routine.” It may be truthful, but it postpones the concentrated help promised. A stronger version names the bounded task and starts the first fold. The remaining footage must actually contain the promised actions; a count is a commitment, not decoration.

Now suppose the source uses a mechanical lever as an analogy for multiplying effort. The packing footage has no equivalent force relationship. Retain the visible action-and-result teaching, but discard the lever explanation. A faithful adaptation preserves the useful relationship rather than every source device.

A second draft says only “fold, place, close.” It is concise but may teach nothing. Add the distinguishing action visible in the footage—for example, which edge aligns with the bag opening. If explaining fabric type would overwhelm a modest introductory promise, leave that advanced detail for another piece and avoid claiming the method works for every garment. The sacrifice is depth; the gain sought is a clear first action.

## Deliverable and checks

For a writing task, deliver the adapted opening and whatever continuation the user requested. For a study or handoff, include the original source locator, sequence, transferable relationship, deliberately changed elements and unresolved factual/asset requirements. Keep useful alternatives available without requiring a large dossier for one hook.

Do not inherit a reference's biography, credentials, metrics or product claims. A widely viewed reference supplies a question worth studying, not a license to call an adaptation proven. When reporting task research, cite the actual source inspected so the user can check the comparison.
