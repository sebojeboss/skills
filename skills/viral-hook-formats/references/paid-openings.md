# Connect an opening to a product argument

## When to read

Use this for product-ad hooks, organic-to-paid adaptation or multiple openings intended to share one continuation. It supplies commercial continuity; it does not replace product research or campaign management.

## How to do the work

Identify the buying reason the body actually supports. A product can have several benefits, but a particular ad may substantiate only one. Read the available body, demonstration, proof, offer and intended action before changing the invitation. If only hooks are requested, use the adequate supplied context and keep the response to hooks; do this compatibility reasoning backstage.

Distinguish attention from buyer relevance. An amusing action can stop a viewer without making the product desirable. A striking metaphor can eventually explain a benefit while withholding product identity too long for this execution. A direct opening can select a narrower but more relevant audience. These are tradeoffs to judge against the actual task, not reasons to optimize every ad for the broadest reach.

Locate product integration accurately:

- The product may be visible before it is named.
- Its name may be spoken before its use is clear.
- A use demonstration may support one claim without proving a larger outcome.
- An offer and CTA require their own truthful conditions.

Preserve the bridge between these events. When changing the opening changes the viewer's motivation, update the supporting body and proof or choose a compatible opening. A **common body** is a continuation shared across variants. Its identical text does not make it suitable for every new invitation. Changing the buying argument and demonstration creates a concept change, not a clean first-line comparison.

Use concrete problem history only when it is real. Genuine previous attempts can explain why the product route matters; invented wasted money or frustration creates a false testimonial. A founder introduction is useful when that identity gives relevant knowledge or stakes. A generic introduction can delay the promised demonstration. No universal ban on introductions or requirement for a confession follows.

A literal pain call-out ("If you struggle with X, watch this") still works often enough to stay common, which is exactly why it reads as an ad. Usually the problem is right and the phrasing is worn. Put the viewer inside the moment instead, the cable sliding behind the desk or the strap leaving a red line by lunch, and only use moments the product actually resolves. Then check that the first image shows what the words say: a line about a crowded desk over a shot of an empty one has already split the invitation.

Immediate offer openings can suit a known product, ready audience or time-sensitive shopping occasion. A discovery audience may first need to understand what is offered and why it matters. Never manufacture urgency or assume seasonal observations establish a year-round rule. Similarly, a skeptical opening must resolve a real doubt rather than invent an accusation to rebut.

Keep product evidence inside the creative. A disclaimer outside a script does not fix an invented result inside it. A self-written “number one,” simulated poll or illustrated review is not independent proof. Do not copy a reference's claimed capacity, clinical effect, savings, guarantee or personal experience without evidence and applicable conditions.

## Worked decisions

Illustrative source: several bags tumble into a cart in a playful animation. A brand has supplied multiple colors and real footage of one bag being packed.

**Option A:** the opening invites a color choice. The body shows the actual options and the offer. The animation can be adapted as an assortment cue. It does not prove preference or scarcity.

**Option B:** the opening asks how the shown travel items fit. This needs the packing demonstration and a continuation about layout. A body showing only colors leaves the new question unanswered. If the user needs variants for that existing color body, choose A or another compatible invitation; do not label B a ready-to-run swap.

A proposed line says the bag holds twice as much as alternatives. The supplied footage does not contain a matched measurement. Remove that multiplier. An opening such as “Here's how these items fit in this bag” can be supported by the actual demonstration without claiming a comparative advantage. This is a truthful scope change, not a disguised performance claim.

A second illustrative draft begins with a long metaphor about a crowded hallway before revealing a cable organizer. If the intended benefit is keeping a desk connection accessible, show that use or identify the object before the metaphor. The metaphor can then enrich an understood point. More spectacle is unnecessary when the missing piece is simply what the object does.

## Deliverable and checks

For a production handoff, supply the spoken opening, overlay, first action, necessary continuation, common-body identifier and material evidence/shot dependency. For multiple variants, use a short comparison table when useful. Mark unavailable footage and unsupported claims explicitly; do not claim the edit has been produced.

Do not infer purchases from organic views, comments or the length of time an ad is publicly visible. Campaign experimentation, attribution and budgets remain with campaign management. Consume the approved offer and destination; do not mutate checkout, publish ads or invent referral URL syntax.
