# Discover adjacent products

## When to read

Find alternatives to an unavailable, unsuitable or crowded seed item, or explore a competitor's relevant assortment.

## How to do the work

Decide what has to survive the move: the buyer job, a useful mechanism, a physical attribute, a style, or a content concept. Then inspect the seed's actual configuration. Follow the competitor's visible categories, collections and related products, and the supplier listing's similar-items section; a promising print or cut often comes in other shapes, colors or sets. Build generic search words from materials, dimensions, shape, function and intended use, and search public catalogs with those.

Branch deliberately. Record the parent candidate and what changes: same claimed model, modified feature, alternative solution or content analogy. Compare the changed feature's benefit and sacrifice. An organizer with a lid may reduce dust but hide contents; a compact version may fit a bag but cease to hold the required object. Return to the buyer constraint before retaining an attractive neighbor.

A discontinued listing remains useful for vocabulary and adjacency. It does not establish availability. Public reverse-image search can help locate visual neighbors when sending that public image is permitted. Do not upload private photos or screenshots automatically. Shared imagery establishes neither a common factory nor identical material.

Treat every new SKU as a reset of product evidence. Reviews, dimensions, durability and sales from the seed do not migrate. “Same problem” reviews can reveal questions about the alternative but cannot substantiate its outcome. Likewise, a successful demonstration format may require different output quality, comfort or access on the new object.

## Worked decisions

Illustrative: a discontinued printed storage basket leads to a similar pattern in a rigid box and then to a hanging organizer. The pattern is common; the jobs are not. The rigid box protects objects but loses foldability. The hanging version frees shelf space but requires a suitable door. Keep all three relationships visible and choose according to the user's actual storage constraint, not visual resemblance.

Illustrative: an adjacent camera accessory suggests a hands-free demonstration. The scene transfers only if the new mount holds steady and the footage actually comes from the offered camera. Reusing the concept does not authorize borrowed footage or prove image quality.

## Deliverable and checks

Return parent, relationship, changed attribute, proposed benefit, sacrifice, exact identity status and evidence that must be reacquired. Do not turn the table into an automatic bundle: first-order complements and later purchases have different jobs and shipping/return costs. Stop when branches cease to preserve the relevant buyer job or only repeat cosmetic variations. Detailed store reconstruction and bundle economics are separate tasks.
