# Returns and supplier remedies

## When to read

Returned/wrong/damaged goods, supplier reimbursement, replacement selection, or deciding whether a return is worth shipping back.

## How to do the work

Open an incident with affected order, variant, quantity, parcel, expected condition and observed defect. Request evidence that distinguishes mispick, transport damage, use/contamination and specification mismatch. Avoid diagnosing cause from one photograph. Identify the actual return destination and who accepts it before promising a supplier-origin return.

Compare remedies on recoverable value: refund/replacement, local repair, return to inspection, returnless resolution or disposal under the applicable policy. Include freight, handling, inspection, uncertainty and time; cheap product cost does not make a long return route sensible. Separate the buyer's remedy from a supplier claim so the customer is not silently held hostage to an unresolved reimbursement.

On receipt, record identity and condition before disposition. Receipt is not resale approval. Wrong contents need custody evidence and a claim; a callback is not resolution. Track supplier credit as promised, issued and reconciled separately. Correction fixes the current goods; corrective action changes the recurring cause. A replacement alone is not evidence the packing or picking process improved. Close the incident only to the level actually established.

## Worked decisions (illustrative)

A returned parcel has the wrong contents. A support callback does not recover the original goods; record the mismatch, custody evidence, claim status and separate account-credit result.

## Deliverable and checks

An [incident packet](supplier-incident.md) with expected/actual condition, evidence list, remedy comparison and unresolved liability/settlement fields. Buyer-facing policies/refunds and messages remain with their owning skills and authorized tools.
