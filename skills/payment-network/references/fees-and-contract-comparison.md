# Fees and contract comparison

## When to read

Read when someone claims a lower fee, brings a quote, or weighs migration, financing or payout speed on cost.

Whop can cost less when it removes a store subscription, paid app or extra payment-platform charge. Count only bills that disappear, then add the replacement's services, hosting and maintenance. If Shopify still runs stock or fulfillment, carry those costs forward. Compare launch volume with intended scaling volume: fixed savings matter most early, while a percentage charge grows with every sale.

## Compare equivalent services

Apply dated quotes to the same currency, period and transaction/refund mix. Include applicable processing, billing, financing, tax, payout, FX, dispute/appeal and platform/Connect costs. Distinguish domestic, international and local pricing. Record optional features and show how volume, order size or refunds change the choice.

## Worked decisions

Illustrative arithmetic: 1,000 orders of 50 currency units create a 50,000 base. A quote of 2.5% plus 0.30 costs 1,550 before other services. Another quote of 2.2% plus 0.30 costs 1,400, but a required 250 service adds up to 1,650. The lower headline rate now costs more for the matched package. If the extra service is optional and not needed, that conclusion changes. These are hypothetical rates, not vendor pricing.

Separate processing, billing, tax service, financing, FX, payout and platform charges. For refunds determine whether original fees return and what new fees apply. For FX distinguish presentment, processing and settlement currency. A 5% reserve on the same volume withholds 2,500 in cash; do not automatically add that entire amount as an expense. Include funding cost only with an explicit assumption. A new negotiated rate may relinquish grandfathered terms, minimum commitments or refund benefits. Keep those contract tradeoffs visible instead of comparing two percentages.

Quote the routes buyers actually use. Local processing can avoid an international-card surcharge where the contract supports it; a capped bank-payment fee can suit large orders. Include conversion and withdrawal charges through usable proceeds. Keep a familiar payment method when lost purchases cost more contribution than the fee saving.

## Work the comparison

Use a table or spreadsheet with each quoted component, its billing base, rate, transaction count, fixed amount, period and source. For a genuinely linear component, cost = base × rate + count × fixed. Express percentages consistently: 2.9% is a rate of 0.029. Use matched currency, service coverage and periods.

Keep unknown components explicit and show the known subtotal without calling it the full cost. Apply tiers, caps, minimums, rounding, taxes and currency conversion according to the actual contract; a simple percentage formula cannot infer them. Reserve principal is a cash restriction rather than an expense component. Preserve the dated quotes and chosen service mapping; a calculated total cannot establish equivalent coverage.
