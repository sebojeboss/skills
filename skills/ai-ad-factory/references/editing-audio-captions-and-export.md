# Editing, audio, captions and export

## When to read

Use for assembly, timing repairs, audio replacement, caption changes or final delivery. A two-word correction should not load the full production process.

## How to do the work

Assemble around meaning and visible action, then tighten idle time. If a tightened cut no longer carries the main message, it's too short, whatever its runtime. Trim in source time before retiming; distinguish source interval from output duration. Preserve a dry/untouched audio master. Room treatment should fit the depicted recording arrangement; it does not fix voice identity, wrong words or pronunciation. If cleanup damages intelligibility, compare the original and repaired stems rather than assuming the processed version is better.

Build captions from verified final speech, using transcription as an aid, not an authority that overrides listening. Preserve raw tokens and record corrections. Normalize punctuation and genuine token splits without inserting missing words. Read all emitted events for spelling and chronology. Measure using the real font; a word-count limit cannot guarantee width. Check headings and body captions across their active intervals for collision with faces, hands and product proof.

Choose a delivery raster/aspect and codec for the actual destination. Review crop, orientation, final-word integrity, silence, color and decode. Watch the export once at phone size with the sound off: you'll catch the misspelled caption your ears were correcting for, and see whether the ad still makes sense to people who never unmute. Then listen once on headphones for level jumps and lips drifting off the words. Avoid scaling up merely to scale down. Keep editable sources while avoiding unnecessary lossy intermediate encodes. Encoder and platform presets are environment-specific; don't import another machine's hardware codec or permanent caption coordinates.

## Worked decisions

Illustrative: a caption fits in a paused opening frame but covers a product lever later. Reflow or move the affected events while preserving approved wording and timing where possible. A global lower caption lane may be convenient but fails when the demonstration occupies it. If speech says the wrong word, silently replacing its caption does not repair the performance.

## Deliverable and checks

Use [media editing and export](media-primitives-contract.md) to plan the operation and verify the rendered file. For montage, ASS/SRT placement, audio mixing, color management and graphic overlays, use the user’s supported editor or a verified local FFmpeg workflow. Export a new version, inspect actual final audio/video where available and report narrower review when not.
