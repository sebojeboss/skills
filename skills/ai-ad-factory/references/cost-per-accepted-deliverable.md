# Cost per accepted deliverable

## When to read

Use for budget comparisons, retry decisions, package costing or production postmortems. A quick copy edit does not require a financial worksheet.

## How to do the work

Choose the denominator first: shot, export, version-specific accepted export or accepted package. Count acceptance against a named contract and reviewer. Do not infer acceptance from the existence of files or a final-shot/generation ratio.

Keep cash, credits, active labor, compute/queue time and calendar time separate. Attribute preparation, generation, selection, repair, editing and finishing consistently. Include discarded work within the declared project scope; refunds reduce cash but do not erase review labor. State how shared assets/subscriptions are allocated. Do not book future reuse savings as realized.

Calculate attributable cash / accepted units only when the denominator is positive and costs are sufficiently reconciled. Report missing charges and unknown labor explicitly. If monetizing labor, show hours and rate separately. Avoid double-counting subscription cash and its allocated credit equivalent. Parallel machine time is not additive human labor.

## Worked decisions

Illustrative arithmetic, not an observed result: $72 attributable cash and three hours of labor produce four exports; two are accepted for the agreed use. Cash cost is $36 per accepted export, with three total hours reported separately. $18 is the cost per generated export and answers a different question. If none are accepted, report $72 spent and zero accepted outputs, not zero unit cost.

For the next repair decision, compare remaining effort, added charges, integration risk and likelihood of preserving required facts. The money already spent does not make another retry economical. A usable fragment from a rejected take can lower new work without converting that entire take into an accepted deliverable.

## Deliverable and checks

Return scope, cost rows/units, acceptance list, allocation, unresolved charges and calculation. Use a spreadsheet or direct decimal arithmetic. Compare like purpose/quality thresholds before ranking providers. This measures production economics, not contribution margin or sales lift.
